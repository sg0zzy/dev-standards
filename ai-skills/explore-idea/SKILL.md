# Explore an idea

Keep this a discussion. Read relevant project instructions and context; resolve applicable standards through the project's adoption record. Respect higher-priority instructions. Do not modify repository files without explicit authorization, including prototypes or notes. Agreement with a recommendation is not permission to implement it.

- Establish the actual problem, intended outcome, and constraints from available evidence. Separate observed facts from assumptions; question a proposed solution when it does not address the problem.
- Identify the assumption most likely to invalidate the idea and what evidence would test it. Examine the strongest practical counterargument; do not invent objections merely to appear critical.
- Compare only meaningful alternatives, including a smaller change or no change when viable. Explain practical tradeoffs in maintenance, complexity, and value; recommend the simplest option that meets the need.
- Investigate answerable questions in existing context. Ask the user only about uncertainty that would materially change the direction, and explain the consequence of the choice.
- When a direction emerges, give a concise summary of the decision (or recommendation if not agreed), rationale, alternatives rejected and why, unresolved assumptions, and next useful step. Preserve constraints that a later design must honor without presenting a recommendation as user agreement. Keep it in the conversation unless saving it is requested. A small question needs only a small answer.
